Three ranked profiles. Both main audiences want the same thing underneath, which is **someone accountable so they stop having to think about it**. The investor version is simply more demanding, so the register changes and the promise does not.

## ICP 1 · Primary, premium

### The multi-property investor

Has capital and does not want to become a full-time landlord. Often time-poor and financially literate, and may already own one or two units. Hires JFC to source, finance, renovate, manage and eventually trade a portfolio, with one advisor who understands loans, tax and the short-term rental rules.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Buys** | Continuity, consistent returns, tax efficiency, discretion, and a relationship measured in years. |
| **Fears** | An advisor who disappears after the transaction. Returns that were never defensible. Being caught out by a rule that changed. |
| **Register** | Premium, corporate, precise. The number first, then the plain explanation. |
| **Where** | Corporate LinkedIn, Jérôme's personal LinkedIn, referrals, the website as a credibility check. |
| **Never** | Lifestyle framing, urgency tactics, or a return figure with no method behind it. This reader will ask how you got the number. |

## ICP 2 · Secondary, volume

### The owner who needs management

Owns an apartment and wants the work of short-term rental handled while staying on the right side of the rules. More practical and more emotional than the investor. Responds to storytelling and reassurance rather than portfolio logic.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Buys** | Peace of mind, hands-off management, and confidence that the paperwork is correct. |
| **Fears** | Bad guests, empty weeks, admin, and being quietly non-compliant without knowing it. |
| **Register** | Warm, human, visual. Show the experience first, then the reassurance. |
| **Where** | Short-term Instagram, the Airbnb co-host profile, Le Bon Coin. |
| **Never** | Tax jargon or portfolio language. This reader owns one flat and does not think of themselves as an investor yet. |

## ICP 3 · Tertiary, funnel feeder

### The one-off buyer or seller

A single transaction today and a possible portfolio relationship in three years. Treat every transaction as the start of a relationship rather than the end of a deal. Reached through sales content, local presence and referrals. No dedicated channel, and no dedicated budget.

## How an investor actually arrives

Most JFC leads currently skip straight to step four through a referral. The job of the content system is to fill steps one to three so strangers can complete the same journey.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Notices">
    Sees a post explaining a rule change or a real client decision. Follows Jérôme rather than the company.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Checks">
    Looks at the profile and the website. Wants proof this is a real operator, not a content account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tests">
    Downloads the guide or replies to a post with a question. Low commitment, high intent.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Talks">
    Books a twenty-minute call about their own numbers. This is where JFC has always converted well.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Where the funnel breaks today.** Steps one to three barely exist. Jérôme converts well once a conversation starts, so the gap is not persuasion, it is reaching people who have never met him.
</Warning>

## Objections, and the line that answers each

| Objection | Who says it | The answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You are small compared to the big platforms | Investor | The platforms that scaled this model went bankrupt, Masteos in 2024 and Bevouac in 2025. Your advisor should still exist in five years. Small is why we are still here. |
| Why not use a specialist for each part | Investor | Because then nobody owns the outcome. Three contacts, three fee structures, and no single person who can tell you whether to sell or keep renting. |
| Can you really prove the returns | Investor | We show the calculation before you commit, including the costs most brochures leave out. If we cannot defend a number, we do not publish it. |
| Short-term letting sounds like a lot of hassle | Owner | It is, which is why we do it. You get the statement and the reviews. We handle everything between the booking and the checkout. |
| I am worried about the new rules | Owner | Reasonable. Most owners are not compliant and do not know it. That is a half-hour conversation, not a crisis. |
| I already have a manager | Owner | Most of our clients did too. The usual reason people move is that nobody was telling them what the property should be earning. |
