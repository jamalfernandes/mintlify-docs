## The two standing rules

<Warning>
  **The returns rule.** A return figure never publishes without its method. This audience will test the number in the first meeting. A target you cannot defend is worse than no target.
</Warning>

<Warning>
  **The consent rule.** No testimonial goes out with a name, a photograph or an identifying detail until that client has agreed **in writing**. This covers case studies and social posts equally.
</Warning>

## Testimonials

The homepage leans on social proof precisely because the brand is Jérôme himself. The wireframe carries two social-proof blocks and both are currently empty.

<Note>
  **Placeholders stay in place until consent arrives.** Do not substitute invented quotes, stock names or composite clients to fill the blocks in the meantime. An empty block is a design problem. A fabricated testimonial is a different kind of problem entirely.
</Note>

What is needed, per testimonial:

<Steps>
  <Step title="The quote itself">
    Verbatim, in the client's own words. The WhatsApp message JFC already holds is strong enough as-is.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Written consent to publish">
    Use [post 13](/posts). A yes or no reply in writing is enough.
  </Step>
  <Step title="An attribution decision">
    First name plus property type, or fully anonymous. The client picks, not JFC.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The same three steps apply to case studies, where the numbers are as identifying as the name.
