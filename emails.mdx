Three sequences, all triggered by something the reader did. The lead magnet asks how many properties someone owns, and that single question routes them into the investor track or the owner track. **Nothing here sends to a purchased list.**

<Columns cols={3}>
  <Card title="A · Investor nurture" icon="scale">
    6 emails over 24 days. Trigger: guide download, two or more properties. Goal: a booked call.
  </Card>

  <Card title="B · Owner enquiry" icon="house">
    4 emails over 12 days. Trigger: guide download with one property, or an enquiry from Instagram, Airbnb or Le Bon Coin. Goal: a management conversation.
  </Card>

  <Card title="C · Network reactivation" icon="users">
    3 emails over 14 days. Trigger: manual, sent in batches to dormant contacts. Goal: restart conversations inside the existing 3,200.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Warning>
  **Before any of this sends.** Analytics and an email platform need to be live, and the guide has to exist as a real document in both languages. The copy is ready. The infrastructure is the dependency. See [Open items](/open-items).
</Warning>

## Sequence A · Investor nurture

The arc runs from useful to personal to direct. **The first three emails ask for nothing**, which is what earns the right to ask in email six.

| # | Subject | Purpose | Day | CTA |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Your guide, and the page to read first | Deliver, set expectations, ask for nothing | 0 | Open the guide |
| A2 | The apartment that was 30,000 euros too expensive | Show judgement through a real decision | 3 | Reply with a question |
| A3 | Short-term earns more. It also costs more. | Prove honesty by naming the downside | 7 | None, reply only |
| A4 | One to buy, one to manage, one to sell | Introduce the positioning explicitly | 12 | See how we work |
| A5 | What we would look at in your portfolio | Make the call concrete before asking for it | 18 | Book a call |
| A6 | Twenty minutes, your numbers | Direct ask, with a clean exit | 24 | Book a call |

    The comparison nobody publishes
    
    Hi [first name],
    
    Short-term letting earns more than long-term across most of Paris. That part is true and most agencies stop there.
    
    Three things the comparison usually leaves out.
    
    Cleaning and linen come out of every booking, not once a year. Vacancy between stays is real, and annual occupancy is always lower than the good months suggest. And somebody has to answer a guest at 9pm, which is either your evening or a management fee.
    
    Once those are in the calculation, short-term still wins for a lot of properties. Not all of them. For a large flat in a quiet area with a long-term tenant already in place, the honest answer is often to leave it alone.
    
    We have told clients that. It costs us the management fee and it keeps the relationship.
    
    Jérôme

## Rules that apply to all three

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Exit conditions">
    A booked call, or a reply that starts a real conversation, removes someone from the sequence immediately. Nobody should receive an automated email the day after speaking to Jérôme.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Suppression">
    No sends to anyone already in another sequence, anyone who unsubscribed, or any active client. Client communication is separate and personal.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Re-entry">
    A contact can enter the investor sequence once. If they later download a different asset, they go to the monthly note instead of restarting.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Sender">
    Everything comes from Jérôme's own address, signed with his first name. A company sender address undoes the accountability the whole brand rests on.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Language routing">
    Sequence language follows the contact's language, captured on the download form. A contact never receives a mixed-language sequence.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Benchmarks

Targets for the first quarter. Volumes will be small at first, so **read the direction rather than the decimal**.

| Metric | A · Investor | B · Owner | C · Reactivation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Open rate | 30 to 40% | 40 to 55% | 25 to 35% |
| Click-through | 4 to 8% | 6 to 12% | 3 to 6% |
| Reply rate | 2 to 5% | 5 to 10% | 4 to 8% |
| Booked calls | 2 to 5% | 3 to 7% | 1 to 3% |
| Unsubscribe | under 1% | under 1% | 1 to 2% |

Opens run higher than standard nurture benchmarks because these lists are small, warm and personally sourced. **Reply rate is the metric that matters most**, since a reply is what actually starts the sale.
