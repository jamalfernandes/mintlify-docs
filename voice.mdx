One personality, four registers. You already do this without thinking. You speak differently in a client meeting than over dinner, and you are still the same person. The accounts work the same way.

## The four registers

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Corporate LinkedIn" icon="briefcase">
    **In the meeting.** Precise and premium. Numbers open the post. No first-person storytelling. Explains, then stops.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Personal LinkedIn" icon="user">
    **Around the office.** Founder voice. First person, real client situations, opinions with reasons. The strongest performing register today.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Short-term Instagram" icon="house">
    **Over dinner.** Warm and visual. Short sentences, concrete details, reassurance. No tax language.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Personal Instagram" icon="camera">
    **Off duty.** Human and light. Builds trust, not leads. Should never contradict the professional accounts.
  </Card>
</Columns>

<Tip>
  **The consistency test.** Before anything publishes, ask whether it could have come from the same person as the last three posts on the other accounts. If not, the register drifted.
</Tip>

## Do and don't

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Do" icon="circle-check">
    Open with the number, the deadline or the outcome.

    Explain any term the moment it appears. LMNP, DPE and loi Le Meur each get half a sentence.

    Name the trade-off. Saying what a strategy costs is what makes the advice credible.

    Use short sentences. Clarity is the promise, so the writing has to model it.

    Write for one reader, not an audience.

    Close with a next step a stranger can take.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Don't" icon="circle-x">
    No em-dashes. Use a period, a comma, or the word _to_ for ranges.

    No hype words. The list is below.

    No promises without proof. _Best service_ and _trusted partner_ are noise, not claims.

    No _premium_ or _exclusive_ as a differentiator. Three competitors already say it.

    No rhetorical questions as openers.

    No advice that would need a lawyer's footnote to be true.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## Words

  <Card title="Use" icon="thumbs-up">

<Warning>

## Eight rewrites

The fastest way to learn the voice is to see the same idea fixed. Left is what most agencies publish. Right is JFC.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="1 · The boilerplate opener">
    **Before.** JFC Immobilier is your trusted partner for premium, seamless real estate solutions that unlock the full potential of your investments.

    **After.** Most agencies sell your flat or manage it. We do both, plus we tell you what to buy next and handle the LMNP tax. One advisor, from the first purchase to the eventual sale.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="2 · The rhetorical question">
    **Before.** Are you making the most of your property investment? Discover our bespoke solutions today.

    **After.** Most owners we meet are earning about 30 percent less than the flat could return. Usually it is pricing, not the property. Send me the address and I will tell you which one it is.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="3 · The guaranteed return">
    **Before.** We offer exclusive, high-yield investment opportunities with guaranteed returns.

    **After.** We target a minimum seven percent return and show you the calculation before you commit, including the costs the brochure leaves out. Some properties do not clear it. We say so.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="4 · The regulation platitude">
    **Before.** Navigating the ever-changing regulatory landscape can be challenging for property owners.

    **After.** The rules changed twice in two years. Most owners are not compliant and do not know it. It takes half an hour to find out which side of the line you are on.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="5 · The dedicated team">
    **Before.** Our dedicated team is committed to delivering excellence at every stage of your journey.

    **After.** You deal with me. I bought the flat with you, I manage it, and I will handle the sale. There is nobody to be passed along to.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="6 · The upside-only pitch">
    **Before.** Short-term rentals are a fantastic way to maximise your rental income!

    **After.** Short-term earns more than long-term in most of Paris. It also costs more in cleaning, in vacancy between bookings, and in the time somebody has to spend on it. Here is the honest comparison.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="7 · The vague credential">
    **Before.** With years of experience and hundreds of satisfied clients, we are the partner of choice.

    **After.** Twelve years in the business, around 40 apartments, and 750 plus clients through the LMNP process. Ask any of them what happens when something goes wrong at 9pm.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="8 · The empty close">
    **Before.** Contact us today to learn more about how we can help you achieve your goals!

    **After.** Tell me what you own and what you want it to earn. Twenty minutes, no charge, and you will leave with a number either way.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Voice in French

The voice rules survive translation. The _rhythm_ does not.

Short sentences are the load-bearing element here, and a literal French pass lengthens almost every one of them. French business writing also defaults to a more formal register than this voice wants. A native writer should keep the sentence length and resist the formality, not smooth it out.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Survives translation" icon="circle-check">
    The proof rule. The structure of the rewrites. The claim-and-pillar logic. The refusal to publish an undefended number.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Needs a native pass" icon="triangle-alert">
    Sentence rhythm. The word lists. _Tu_ versus _vous_ (see [Open items](/open-items)). Anything that reads as an idiom.
  </Card>
</Columns>
